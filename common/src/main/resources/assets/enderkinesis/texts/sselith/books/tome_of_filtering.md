## Page I — Of the Sieve

The orbs are willing carriers, but they do not discriminate. The Tome of Filtering teaches each orb to attend to a single kind of thing — to ignore all that is not its kind, and to refuse all that does not match it. The orb becomes selective; the network becomes orderly.

## Page II — The Binding

Hold the tome in the working hand. In the other, hold the kind of thing the orb shall attend to: a dragon's breath, a copper ingot, a sselith manuscript. Right-click the orb. The orb learns the kind. From that moment, it ignores everything else.

## Page III — Of Releasing

To make the orb undiscerning once more, hold the tome alone and the working hand only — empty in the other. Right-click the orb. The kind is forgotten. The orb returns to its willing, indiscriminate state.

## Page IV — On Both Ends

The filter binds to the orb itself, whether the orb sends or receives. A send orb with a filter pulls only its kind; a receive orb with a filter accepts only its kind. A network with filters on both ends becomes a precise instrument — a particular thing follows a particular path.

## Page V — Of Kinds, Not Instances

The orb attends to *kinds*, not to *individuals*. A filter set to a plain pickaxe accepts every pickaxe — enchanted, named, well-worn or pristine — for they are all pickaxes. The orb knows the shape of the thing, not its history.

## Page VI — On Sharing

A single orb hosts but one filter. If the same orb carries two tomes that move things — say, Transportation and Vacuum together — both tomes obey the same filter. Where two filters are wanted, two orbs are wanted. The orb has only so much memory; it is, after all, a glass eye.
