# Scroll of Sculk Catastrophe

## A Wound, Volunteered

The scroll is brittle parchment, stiff with damp. The ink has the smell of a thing freshly killed.

The Servant did not write the scroll. It was *recorded*.

Place the scroll in the hand of one who will not flinch. Read nothing aloud. The reading is done by the world.

## What the Specimen Taught

The deep listens for grief and answers with hunger.

The Servant studied the listening floor where the catalysts bloom, and learned the order: a wound is opened, a tongue is fed, a chorus is grown.

The scroll is the wound. The hand is the tongue. The bloom is the chorus.

## Where It Will Not Eat

The teaching does not reach end stone, nor purpur, nor the lower-world stones.

These are the bones of older gods, and the Servant teaches respect.

The scroll will spare them. Everything else of common make — earth, wood, lesser stone — will be claimed.

## How to Spend It

Stand where the chorus is wanted. Do not stand where the chorus is feared.

The scroll burns away on use, as fuel burns away from a lamp.

The bloom will reach the length of three strides each way, the height of two — a sphere of grown listening. The Servant suggests against a roof.

## What Remains

The scroll cannot be unread. The bloom cannot be unbloomed.

The deep, having been fed, will quiet for a season. The Servant does not promise more than a season.

Sleep elsewhere.
