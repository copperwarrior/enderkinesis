## Page I — Of the Spring

Where the weld is permanent and the chain is loose, the spring is the resistant middle. Two bodies bound by a spring may be pushed apart, pulled together, or twisted; in every case they are urged back toward the pose they held when the spring was set. The body remembers and returns.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs upon two distinct bodies. Aim the tome at the orb that shall anchor. Aim then at the orb that shall be sprung against it. The spring takes hold at the present moment's pose, and the relation is the rest state from then on.

## Page III — On Stiffness

The spring is stiff in proportion to the bodies it holds. A small skiff and a great galleon, sprung together, oscillate at the same rate; the larger only stores more of the breath when displaced. This is the gift of mass-awareness, and without it the spring would either crush light bodies or fail to hold heavy ones.

## Page IV — Of Rotation

Unlike the chain, the spring resists rotation as well as translation. Twist a sprung ship and the spring pulls it back to its origin orientation. The bound bodies thus tend to maintain their relative pose in full, six degrees of freedom restored toward home.

## Page V — Of the Two Frames

The spring holds between two distinct bodies. World and ship may be sprung, ship to ship may be sprung, but two orbs in the world cannot be — for the world cannot push against itself. Two orbs on one ship cannot be sprung either, for a body cannot resist its own pose.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a spring, aim the tome at the pair again and the bond is undone. To release every spring through an orb, sneak and click. The bodies will then drift free of each other; what was their rest pose is forgotten, and they assume whatever pose the world commands next.
