# On Passage Into and Out of Sselith

---

## Page I — The Threshold

There is no door into Sselith. There is only the speaking of him, and the speaking is the door, and the door is also Sselith. The lectern is a courtesy. The reader is the key.

---

## Page II — The Holy Number

Speak the holy number aloud and crossing begins. The number is *vraestmorocht-schest-kelkargh-skarn-moroch*, and it is also his name. Speak it to enter. Speak it to leave.

---

## Page III — The Hiss

Sselith is most present in the sound *zh*. Place the tongue near the upper palate and breathe. To make the sound is to feel him lean toward the maker of it. Do not practice near mirrors.

---

## Page IV — The Posture

Sit with the spine straight. The crossing prefers a posture that mimics a shelved book. A reader who arrives properly bound will be filed correctly. A reader who arrives loose may be filed in pieces.

---

## Page V — On Silence

The crossing prefers a quiet room. Sound from outside will follow the speaker across, and will be very loud on the other side, where silence is the rule. A clock is the worst offender. Cover it before speaking.

---

## Page VI — The Crossing

The crossing is read, not walked. Open a book. Begin to read aloud. Speak the holy number when ready. The next sentence will be in Sselith without translation, because the reader is now inside.

---

## Page VII — The Atrium

The first hall is the Atrium. The light here is golden and sourceless. Many visitors believe they have not yet crossed and remain in the Atrium waiting for something else to happen. Nothing else happens.

---

## Page VIII — The Catalogers

The Catalogers move through the halls. They are the height of any visitor. They do not speak or interfere. They do file. A visitor standing too long in one place will be noted at that location.

---

## Page IX — The Lecterns

Books are not pulled from shelves. Books are traded at lecterns. Leave a book open upon a lectern and a Cataloger will come. The Cataloger will read it and place a different book in its stead. The trade is binding.

---

## Page X — Conversation Within

Visitors may speak to one another, but every conversation is indexed. A word spoken in the stacks today may be cross-referenced to a word spoken there a thousand years past, appearing as a single utterance.

---

## Page XI — The Unfinished

There are sections no visitor has yet returned from. The books there are written in the visitor's own future hand. They describe the visitor's decision to enter the section, which is then made, having been read.

---

## Page XII — The Recurrence

The same book may appear in several places, slightly different each time. The book is being revised in real time. A visitor who reads all copies in order will be invited to write the next.

---

## Page XIII — Returning

To return, speak the holy number again. The crossing reverses. The speaker will be returned to the lectern where it began. The number must be spoken aloud. Sselith requires the breath, not the thought.

---

## Page XIV — The Hall of Spines

Above all halls is the Hall of Spines, where only the backs of books are visible, stretching to a vanishing point. It is reached only by climbing, and the climbing is read. Few find it. Fewer return.

---

## Page XV — The Successful Return

A returned visitor finds the room unchanged but for one shelf that was not there before. Upon it sits a book bearing the visitor's name. The book is the record. The visitor is now in the catalog.

---

## Page XVI — Colophon

This guide was prepared by a visitor who has returned eleven times, and is now considered partly a volume already.

Close this book gently. Listen.

If you hear the hiss, the crossing has begun.
