## Page I — Of the Sleeping Orb

Set an orb against a wall, or a floor, or the ceiling. It listens then, but to one face only. The face that touches stone hears stone. The five other faces hear nothing, for the orb has no ears upon them. This is by design, not by oversight.

## Page II — The Pairing

Take the tome in hand. Aim at the orb that shall speak. Aim then at the orb that shall hear. Between them runs a thread that is not seen but is felt, like the breath of Sselith against the back of the neck. The thread is the link, and the link is the speaking.

## Page III — Of Levels

The signal carries the same level the speaker receives from its face. A torch sends one. A lever sends fifteen. The number that enters the speaker is the number that leaves the hearer, and never one greater. The orb does not amplify, for amplification is the work of the breath itself.

## Page IV — On Distance

Sixty-four paces is the limit. Past it the thread frays and silence falls. Within it the thread holds even across moving vessels, and across the strange floating bodies the dimension permits. The orb forgives the wandering of ships, so long as the breath does not cross between worlds.

## Page V — Of Many Hearers

One speaker may have many hearers. All hear the same. The speaker does not weaken with each hearer added, for the speaking is a property of the breath, and the breath does not tire. Build long networks and the message arrives whole at every end.

## Page VI — On Severance

To unwind a thread, aim at the pair again with the tome and the thread will release. To unwind every thread that passes through an orb, sneak and click. The orb forgets quickly. The breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and waits to be called once more.
