## Page I — Of the Rigid Rod

Between the rope that holds only when stretched, and the weld that holds in every direction, lies the rod that holds only the distance. So is the coupling made. The orbs cannot draw closer, cannot draw farther; but they may turn about the line that joins them as freely as a wheel turns upon its axle.

## Page II — The Pairing

Aim the tome at the orb that shall anchor. Aim then at the orb that shall be held. The distance between them at that moment becomes the distance the coupling will always hold. It will not lengthen if pulled, nor shorten if pushed. Place the orbs at the distance you wish before you link.

## Page III — Of Rotation

Though the length is fixed, the angles are not. A coupled ship may swing about a world anchor as a pendulum swings; two coupled ships may pivot one upon the other as a hand turns about a wrist. The coupling does not care which way they face, only how far apart they sit.

## Page IV — Of the Difference From Other Bindings

The Chain holds only the far limit and is slack within. The Weld holds every angle and every distance. The Spring permits the distance to flex and to recover. The Coupling holds the distance alone, and lets the rest of the motion run free. Each tome has its place; choose by the kind of motion you wish to keep.

## Page V — Of Forbidden Pairings

A coupling needs two bodies. Two orbs both pinned to the world cannot be coupled, for the world is a single body. Two orbs on the same ship are likewise refused. Use Coupling across vessels, or across a vessel and the still world.

## Page VI — On Severance

Aim the tome at the pair again to release the coupling. Sneak and click to release every coupling that passes through this orb. The rod dissolves at once; the bodies are loosed; the breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and waits to be called once more.
