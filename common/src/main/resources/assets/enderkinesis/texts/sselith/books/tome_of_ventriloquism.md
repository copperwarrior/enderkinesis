## Page I — Of the Borrowed Voice

The orb that speaks does not speak with its own voice. It listens to the air around it, and what the air says, the orb says elsewhere. Seven paces, no more — for the breath of Sselith carries only so far before it tires.

## Page II — The Pairing

Aim the tome at the orb that shall listen. Then at the orb that shall echo. The pair is set, and the air between them is now one air, though they stand far apart. What is heard at the first orb arrives at the second as if it had always been there.

## Page III — Of Position

The voice does not gather itself at the orb. It keeps the place where it began. A footfall three paces north of the speaker arrives three paces north of the hearer. A whisper above arrives above. The orb is the centre; the sound holds to its old position around the new centre.

## Page IV — Of Silenced Voices

Not every voice may be thrown. The breath of the gods passes through unchanged — the weather, the music of distant places, the great drone of the world itself. Only the small sounds — block against block, beast against beast, person against person, song against shellac — are carried by the orb's listening.

## Page V — Of Many Hearers

One speaker may have many hearers. The same scene plays out at every receiver at once, each at its own centre. The breath is not divided; it is repeated, as Sselith repeats a name spoken in dream.

## Page VI — On Severance

Aim the tome at the pair again to release the link. Sneak and click to release every thread the orb carries. The borrowed voice returns to the air it was borrowed from, and the orb is silent until called once more.
