## Page I — Of the Winch

Where the chain holds at a length, the winch shortens it. The same rope, the same two orbs — but now a lever upon the wall commands the distance. Lift the lever and the bodies are drawn together; lower it and they drift back to where the rope began.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs on distinct bodies. Aim the tome at the orb that shall pull. Aim then at the orb that shall be pulled. The rope is set at the present distance; from then on the signal at the puller's face decides how short the rope is permitted to be.

## Page III — Of Power

The signal speaks in fifteen grades. At zero, the rope is at its full length, slack inside, taut at the limit. At fifteen, the rope is reeled to its smallest practical length, the bodies drawn as close as the breath permits without violence. Between these, the rope shortens by even fractions.

## Page IV — On Smoothness

The pull is a spring, not a snap. When the signal climbs, the bodies are drawn in over the count of several seconds rather than wrenched together in an instant. A heavy ship being pulled by a light one feels the strain longer; a small skiff at the same setting arrives more quickly. The breath sizes itself to the load.

## Page V — Of the Limits

The pulled body cannot pass into the puller — a small distance is always kept between them. The two cannot be on the same ship, for a ship cannot reel itself in; nor can both be in the world, for the world has no winches.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a winch, aim the tome at the pair once more and the bond is undone. The rope falls and the bodies move freely again. To release every winch through an orb, sneak and click. The breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and waits for the lever to ask of it once more.
