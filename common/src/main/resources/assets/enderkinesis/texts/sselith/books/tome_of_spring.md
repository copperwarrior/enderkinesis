## Page I — Of the Coil and the Breath

The breath of Sselith does not only bind, and does not only hold loosely. It can also be wound, like a coil of metal that remembers its shape. Push the coil, and it pushes back. Pull the coil, and it pulls back. So is the spring made between two orbs.

## Page II — The Pairing

Aim the tome at the orb that shall anchor. Aim then at the orb that shall be sprung. The coil takes its measure at the moment of pairing — the distance between them then is the distance the coil will always remember. Place the orbs at the distance you wish to keep before linking; this cannot be changed later.

## Page III — Of the Rest Distance

When the bodies are at the remembered distance, the coil is silent and does no work. Push them closer together, and the coil bears them apart. Pull them farther, and the coil draws them in. The coil does not tire, for it is made of breath, and breath does not tire.

## Page IV — Of Rotation

The coil constrains only the distance between the orbs, not the angles. The bodies may turn freely around the line that joins them; the coil holds the length without holding the orientation. So a sprung ship may spin upon its tether, and a sprung world-anchor lets the ship pivot as it bounces.

## Page V — Of Forbidden Pairings

A coil needs two bodies. Two orbs both pinned to the world cannot be sprung, for the world is a single body and a coil cannot wind itself against itself. Two orbs on the same ship are likewise refused. Use Spring across vessels, or across a vessel and the still world.

## Page VI — On Severance

Aim the tome at the pair again to release the coil. Sneak and click to release every coil that passes through this orb. The breath unwinds at once; the bodies cease to bounce; the coil returns to Sselith, which is its source, and waits to be called once more.
