## Page I — Of the Tether

A chain is not a weld. The bound bodies move freely within the chain's length, and only at the length itself does the chain pull them back. Inside the slack the orbs are as untethered as any other; at the limit they remember each other.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs upon two distinct bodies. Aim the tome at the orb that shall be the anchor. Aim then at the orb that shall be tethered. The chain takes its length from the distance between them at that very moment, and that length is fixed.

## Page III — On Length

To make a longer chain, place the orbs farther apart before linking. To make a shorter one, place them closer. The tome does not stretch or shorten an existing chain. If the length is wrong, undo it and bind again.

## Page IV — Of the Two Frames

The chain holds only between two distinct bodies. The world and a ship may be chained, and the ship will then drift only so far before it is pulled back. Two ships may be chained, and they will keep their distance through every storm. Two orbs in the world cannot be chained, for there is no tension between them; the world does not strain against itself.

## Page V — The Soft Limit

When the chain meets its limit it does not snap. It holds, and the bodies hold with it. A ship pulled hard against its chain feels the resistance and slows. The reader will see the orbs at the chain's ends remain at their distance no matter the force, until the chain itself is released.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a chain, aim the tome at the pair once more and the bond is undone. To release every chain through an orb, sneak and click. The freed bodies will then move as their own forces dictate, with no memory of the chain that bound them.
