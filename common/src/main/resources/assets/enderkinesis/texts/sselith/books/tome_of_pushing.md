## Page I — Of the Strut

Where the winch shortens a rope, the strut lengthens it. The same two orbs, the same captured length — but where the lever is lifted, the bodies are urged apart instead of together. Lower the lever and the strut goes slack, and the rope is a rope once more.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs on distinct bodies. Aim the tome at the orb that shall push. Aim then at the orb that shall be pushed. The rope is set at the present distance; from then on the signal at the pusher's face decides the minimum separation the bodies are permitted to hold.

## Page III — Of Power

The signal speaks in fifteen grades, and each grade is one pace of compulsion. A signal of three keeps the bodies at least three paces apart. A signal of fifteen keeps them at least fifteen. At zero, no minimum is enforced and the rope behaves as it would without the strut.

## Page IV — On Smoothness

The push is a spring, not a shove. When the signal climbs, the bodies are urged apart over the count of several seconds rather than slammed apart in an instant. A heavy ship pushing a light one finds the light one moves first; both ships pushing each other meet in the middle of the gap. The breath sizes itself to the load.

## Page V — Of the Limits

The two cannot be on the same ship, for a ship cannot push itself away from itself; nor can both be in the world, for the world has no struts. The minimum cannot exceed fifteen paces, since the signal cannot speak louder than that.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a strut, aim the tome at the pair once more and the bond is undone. The bodies move freely again, and the rope is gone with the strut that held it. To release every strut through an orb, sneak and click. The breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and waits for the next signal.
