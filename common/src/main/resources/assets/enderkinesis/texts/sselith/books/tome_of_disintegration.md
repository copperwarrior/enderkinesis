## Page I — Of the Lance

Where the transport tome carries a thing from one place to another, the disintegration tome carries a thing **into being a thing that can be carried**. The beam between the orbs is not a passive thread; it is a slow, patient unmaking, and what falls into it is undone — into its components, into its drops — and the receiver finds those drops where there had been something else.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs. Aim the tome at the orb that shall be the source of the lance; aim then at the orb that shall be its terminus. The line between them is the beam, and the beam is the unmaking.

## Page III — Of the Mining

The beam eats the first solid block it touches and no other. When that block has been entirely consumed — at a pace set by the block's own stubbornness, so soft earth yields quickly and obsidian endures — the beam advances to the next block in its path. Like a drill in a tunnel, the lance progresses one mouthful at a time. Drops are gathered and dispatched to the receiver as if a transportation tome had taken them.

## Page IV — Of the Damage

Living things caught between the orbs are damaged for as long as they remain in the beam's path. The beam does not distinguish between mob and master, and the patient scholar should not stand in their own work. Mob drops, like block drops, are collected and dispatched to the receiver — the lance is a working butcher as well as a working miner.

## Page V — On the Bedrock

Some blocks are made of the world's own bones and cannot be unmade by lances or by any other instrument. When the beam strikes such a block it stops there, unable to see past, and waits. The path resumes only when the bedrock is gone — which, in this world, means: never. Plan the lance's run accordingly.

## Page VI — Of the Receiver

The receiver's container holds whatever the beam produces — block drops, mob loot, items the beam catches mid-flight. If the container is full, the beam still mines and still damages, but the drops accumulate within the tome's memory until room appears. The lance does not rest; it is the receiver that must be tended.

## Page VII — On Severance

To release a beam, aim the tome at the pair once more and the bond is undone. To release every beam through an orb, sneak and click. The lances fall silent. The drops cease to flow. The breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and the orbs cool to nothing more than glass once again.
