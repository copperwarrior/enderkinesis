## Page I — Of the Pivot

Where the bond welds two ships together, the hinge teaches them to pivot. The line between the two orbs becomes the axis of rotation: each ship turns about that line, free to swing as a door swings on its jamb. The orbs are the hinge's pins; the ships are its leaves.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs upon distinct ships. Aim the tome at the orb that shall be the hinge's pivot. Aim then at the orb that shall be its mate. The line between the two becomes the axis; the ships are bound to that axis and may rotate around it, but no further.

## Page III — Of Resistance

The hinge is willing or stubborn at the player's command. A signal at the pivot's face commands its stiffness: at zero, the hinge swings freely as if oiled, and the ships pivot at the slightest push. At fifteen, the hinge is held fast and resists all but the strongest force. Between these, the resistance climbs in even measure.

## Page IV — On the Axis

The axis is set the moment the pair is forged, and it stays where the orbs were when the pair was made. If you wish the hinge to lie along another line, undo the bond and forge it anew with the orbs in their new places. A second forging is forgiving; the breath of Sselith does not begrudge correction.

## Page V — Of the Limits

The two cannot be on the same ship, for a ship cannot hinge against itself. Nor can both be in the world, for the world has no rotation to give. The hinge needs two bodies and a line between them.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a hinge, aim the tome at the pair once more and the bond is undone. To release every hinge through an orb, sneak and click. The breath returns to Sselith, which is its source, and the ships are free to drift apart as they please.
