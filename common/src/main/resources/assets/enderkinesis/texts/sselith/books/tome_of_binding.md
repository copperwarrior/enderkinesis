## Page I — Of the Weld

To bind is to declare that two bodies are now one body. The bodies do not become one in fact, for matter resists such honesty. They become one in obligation: where the first moves, the second moves; where the first turns, the second turns. The fiction is enforced by the breath.

## Page II — The Pairing

Place two orbs. Aim the tome at the orb that shall lead. Aim then at the orb that shall follow. The weld takes hold at the pose held in that moment, and the relation between the two bodies is fixed forever after, until released.

## Page III — Of the World

The world itself is a body, and orbs placed in the world may be bound to orbs placed on ships. The ship will then hold its world-place as though nailed to nothing. The reverse is also true. Two orbs both placed in the world cannot be bound, for the world cannot be bound to itself.

## Page IV — Two Orbs, One Ship

An orb cannot be bound to another orb upon its own ship. The tome will refuse, and rightly. A body cannot constrain itself; the act has no meaning. Place the orbs on different bodies, or do not place them at all.

## Page V — Of Stress

The weld holds against forces the breath alone could not. Push against it and the bound bodies resist together. The orb does not break under load, nor does the weld, for the tome's purpose is permanence within the bounds of the world. Only the holder of the tome may release a weld they have set.

## Page VI — On Severance

To release a weld, aim the tome at the pair again and the bond is undone. To release every weld through an orb, sneak and click. The bodies will then drift apart, or fall, or do whatever the world commands of unbound things. The choice and the consequence are the reader's.
